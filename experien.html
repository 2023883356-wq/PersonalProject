HOW IT ALL BEGAN

  • My working journey started during the PKP period, when I decided to try doing part-time work for the first time. My first job was as a shoe promoter at a footwear shop, and I stayed there for around 7 to 8 months. That job taught me a lot — I had to be fast, alert, and avoid mistakes, especially when taking shoe stocks for customers. I even memorised the shoe arrangement so I could quickly find the right size without making customers wait too long.

  • One of the biggest challenges was the language barrier. I worked under a Chinese supervisor and many of my coworkers were foreigners, so communication was not always easy. Still, I managed to adapt and learned how to work efficiently despite the difficulties.

  • After PKP ended, I decided to resign and explore other experiences. My next job was a one-day part-time position as an event crew. I handled registration and door gifts, checked participant attendance, and explained the event’s tentative schedule when needed. The work wasn’t very challenging because the tasks were straightforward, but it gave me a new experience and exposure to event operations.

POST-SPM JOURNEY

  • A week after finishing my SPM exams, I immediately started job hunting from shop to shop, and eventually I was hired at Pusat Pakaian Hari-Hari for seven months. For me, that was quite a long time, and I learned a lot during that period. For the first four months, I worked as a promoter—serving customers, helping them choose clothing, and making sure the store stayed neat and organized. Hari-Hari also had store supervisors who monitored daily sales performance, so I always had to stay alert and aware of our targets.

  • For the last three months, I was given the opportunity to become a cashier. Even though I was assigned to the counter, I still handled promoter tasks when needed. As a cashier, I had to be more friendly, suggest additional items to customers, and manage payments carefully. Working there opened my eyes to the real world of employment—some environments can be toxic, some colleagues are amazing, and sometimes the pressure from higher-ups can be tough. Still, I was grateful because my coworkers were kind, and I had many memorable moments with them.

  • After leaving Hari-Hari, I continued my studies. During my semester break, I worked at an old currency shop in Pasar Seni for two months as a customer assistant. The shop sold various old Malaysian banknotes and coins, and some customers came to sell their collections to us. The challenging part of this job was the bargaining—negotiating with customers who wanted to sell their old money. I learned how to use the right words so customers would feel confident and comfortable dealing with us. In this job, we mostly bought items rather than sold them, as the shop owner was a collector who was really passionate about old currency.

INTERSHIP EXPERIENCES

  • Before starting my actual internship, I had a short part-time experience as a pengawas pertandingan at Sekolah Agama AU4. They were short on staff, so I was called in as a replacement. The job was simple: before the competition started, the supervisors needed to collect the exam papers, bring them into the hall, and wait for the students to arrive. I guided them to sit according to their seat numbers, distributed the question papers, and monitored them for the whole two-hour session. After everything was done, I just returned the papers and went home. It was an easy but interesting experience for me.

  • For my real internship, I was placed at the Institut Koperasi Malaysia (IKMa) under the Pusat Teknologi Maklumat dan Komunikasi (ICT) for eight weeks. This was my very first time working in a government environment—quite different from my previous jobs in retail and customer service. I had to adapt to a more professional setting and learn how to handle tasks more formally.

  • The ICT department functions as a learning and training centre where they offer various courses for cooperatives. As an intern, my main responsibility was to create e-learning modules for their courses, ensuring that the content was neat, structured, and easy to follow. They also handled many programs and events, and I was lucky enough to get the chance to join and assist in some of them under the ICT team. Overall, the internship taught me a lot about professionalism, teamwork, and how government organisations operate.
