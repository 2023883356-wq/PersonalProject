MY ROOTS AND THE PEOPLE WHO RAISED ME

  • I come from a small family of six, and I am the third child out of four siblings. My mother is originally from Kelantan and my father from Kedah, but all of us—except my second sibling who was born in Kedah—were born in Kuala Lumpur. Both of my parents moved to KL for work and have been teachers for more than 20 years, which means I grew up in a household that truly values education, discipline, and respect.

  • Growing up, my family environment was quite balanced—loving but firm. When I was younger, my parents were a little strict, especially because they worked long hours from morning until evening. I wasn't allowed to go out freely, and most of the time I had to learn to take care of myself. After school, I would come home and prepare everything on my own before going to my religious school. Because of that, I learned independence at a very young age. My mum always reminded me to be disciplined, responsible, and respectful in everything I do.

  • Even though my parents come from Kelantan and Kedah, I grew up speaking KL Malay, so I don’t naturally use either dialect. But whenever we visit our relatives in Kelantan or Kedah, that’s where I learn to understand their dialects—from my grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins. Living in KL shaped me into a city girl through and through. I love the convenience, the fast-paced lifestyle, and the energy of city life, but I also appreciate the peace and beauty of kampung areas. Still, if I had to choose, I would always pick the city.

  • Growing up in a teacher household also meant my parents always encouraged me to follow their footsteps and become a teacher too. For them, education is powerful—it can take you far in life, open new paths, and protect you from many things. No matter what, education will always come first in their eyes, and that belief has shaped who I am today.

And from all these backgrounds, came memories that still stay in my heart until today.

MOMENTS THAT MADE US CLOSE

Childhood Memories

  • Growing up, most of my Raya memories were spent in Kelantan. I don’t know why, but Kelantan always felt more calming and meaningful to me compared to Kedah—maybe because the vibe felt warmer, or maybe because everyone seemed extra cheerful there. Those balik kampung trips were the moments that shaped my love for family gatherings.
    • School Memories
  • When I was younger, my father used to send me to kindergarten and primary school every morning. After I got older, I started going on my own, but the memory of him waiting outside the gate still stays with me.
  • My mom also played a huge role in my studies—she always prayed for me, and I truly believe a mother’s prayer is the strongest form of support. Whenever I needed books or extra learning tools, they never hesitated to provide. My dad even sent me to tuition, although I’ve always preferred studying on my own. Sometimes I didn’t understand things even when a teacher explained it, but I never gave up—I would ask my friends to teach me until I caught up.
  • My mom was very strict about education, yes, rotan moments were real! But looking back now, it’s funny when I see her using the same tone with my younger sibling. It reminds me of my own “kena marah” era.

Sometimes the simplest things bring the most peace — here are the tastes and places that make me feel at home.

THE LOVE THAT I GOT

Growing Up Moments

  • As I grew older, the relationship with my family changed. I’m not someone who shares feelings easily—I usually stay quiet and keep things to myself. Every time I came home, I preferred to be alone in my room. But when I started my diploma and lived far away, things felt different. I became emotional whenever my parents asked about my life there. I would avoid telling them sad things because I knew I would cry, so I tried to answer in the most neutral way. Even so, the distance made me appreciate them more. My parents continued supporting me— my dad provided everything I needed, and my mom prepared all my essentials. Being independent wasn’t hard because they raised me that way, but being far from them made me realise how important they truly are.

A Moment of Realisation

  • When I started missing home, I regretted not being the type to always talk or share with my family. After entering diploma, I found myself going home more often. Each time, I would sit and tell my parents about funny things that happened at college. They would smile and laugh—and those small moments made me feel warm and grateful.

Recent Memories

  • Nowadays, our memories are simple but meaningful: balik kampung trips, walking around festivals, or just spending time together at home. My family rarely eats out because my mom loves cooking, so eating together at home feels extra special. Birthdays are usually celebrated with a cake—nothing fancy, but full of love. What I cherish the most are the moments when we talk, laugh, and enjoy each other’s company. Those are the memories that remind me how lucky I am to have them.
